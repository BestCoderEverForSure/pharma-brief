## Top Stories

### 1. [FTC Settles with CVS Caremark on Insulin Pricing](https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/14/ftc-settles-lawsuit-with-cvs-caremark-over-charges-it-manipulated-insulin-prices-impeded-access/)
**What happened:** The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) settled a lawsuit against CVS Caremark, alleging the pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) artificially inflated insulin prices and impeded access to the diabetes treatment. The settlement requires CVS Caremark to stop preferring higher-cost versions of drugs on standard formularies [1] [6].
**What it means:** This action, similar to a prior settlement with Express Scripts, signals continued regulatory scrutiny on PBM practices. It aims to increase transparency and competition, potentially leading to lower out-of-pocket costs for patients, especially for critical medicines like insulin.
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - The FTC is actively shaping PBM conduct. · *Commercial* - Could shift formulary design and drug purchasing dynamics.

### 2. [US FDA approves Celcuity's breast cancer drug](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/us-fda-approves-celcuitys-breast-cancer-drug-2026-07-14/)
**What happened:** The FDA approved Celcuity's REVTORPYK™ (gedatolisib) for hormone receptor-positive (HR+), HER2-negative (HER2-) locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer. The drug is seen as a potential blockbuster [16] [17] [83].
**What it means:** This approval provides a new treatment option for a significant patient population and positions Celcuity as a compelling acquisition target for larger pharmaceutical companies looking to enhance their oncology portfolios with a high-potential asset [17].
**Lenses:** *Scientific* - Offers a new therapeutic pathway for a common cancer. · *Strategic* - Could trigger M&A interest in Celcuity.

### 3. [AstraZeneca pays up to $1.5bn to license Dizal Pharma’s lung cancer drug](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/astrazeneca-license-lung-cancer-drug-chinas-dizal-pharmaceutical-2026-07-14/)
**What happened:** AstraZeneca has entered an exclusive licensing agreement with China's Dizal Pharmaceutical for global rights to its epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) lung cancer drug, Zegfrovy, for up to $1.5 billion. Zegfrovy is already approved in China [12] [45] [109] [212] [236].
**What it means:** This deal strengthens AstraZeneca's leading oncology pipeline, particularly in lung cancer, and underscores the increasing trend of Western pharmaceutical giants in-licensing innovative assets from Chinese biotechs to expand their global reach and portfolio.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Bolsters AZ's oncology leadership. · *Financial* - Significant upfront and milestone payments for Dizal. · *Geopolitical* - Highlights growing cross-border pharma collaborations with China.

## Eli Lilly Spotlight
- **Retevmo Gains Full Approval:** Eli Lilly's Retevmo (selpercatinib) received traditional FDA approval for RET-fusion positive solid tumors across multiple cancer types, expanding its market access beyond accelerated approval [22] [57].
- **Teva Lawsuit Revived:** A U.S. appeals court revived a Teva Pharmaceuticals lawsuit accusing Eli Lilly of breaching an agreement related to Teva's generic version of Forteo [9]. This introduces new legal uncertainty for Lilly's bone-building drug.
- **Leqembi SC Approved:** Competitor Eisai and Biogen secured FDA approval for an at-home subcutaneous (SC) initiation dose for Leqembi (lecanemab) for early Alzheimer's disease [30] [61]. This could enhance patient convenience and uptake for a key rival in the Alzheimer's space, potentially impacting Lilly's donanemab.
