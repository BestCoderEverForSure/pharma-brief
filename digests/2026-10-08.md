## Top Stories

### 1. [Merck's Subcutaneous Keytruda Blocked in Europe Following Patent Dispute](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/07/merck-keytruda-setback-europe/)
**What happened:** A Dutch court has issued an injunction preventing Merck from manufacturing and marketing its subcutaneous formulation of Keytruda in several European countries due to a patent dispute [2] [7]. This ruling follows a challenge from Halozyme Therapeutics, which claims patent infringement [2] [108].
**What it means:** This is a significant setback for Merck's strategy to extend Keytruda's lifecycle and maintain market share against biosimilars by offering a more convenient subcutaneous option. The decision could benefit Halozyme and potentially impact Merck's competitive positioning in the lucrative European oncology market.

### 2. [Biotech Funding Climate Challenging, But Large Funds Still Active](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/07/caribou-halts-car-t-work-lay-off-staff-amid-challenging-funding-climate/)
**What happened:** Caribou Biosciences announced it would halt its CAR-T cell therapy programs and lay off staff, citing a "challenging" funding climate despite having a late-stage allogeneic cell therapy candidate [1] [18]. This comes as smaller biotech firms are also reportedly struggling to navigate new import tariffs imposed by the Trump administration [5] [20]. In contrast, European venture capital firm Forbion raised $2.6 billion (€2.3 billion), its largest fundraise to date, with Eli Lilly among its new backers [8] [139] [187].
**What it means:** The biotech sector is experiencing a polarized funding environment. While innovative early-stage companies like Caribou face difficulties securing capital for clinical advancement, larger, more established venture funds are successfully raising substantial amounts, likely targeting more mature or de-risked assets. Policy changes like tariffs add further complexity and cost pressure for smaller players.

### 3. [FDA Approves Pfizer's Tukysa for Breast Cancer and Novartis' Rhapsido for Skin Condition](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-approves-tukysa-plus-trastuzumab-and-pertuzumab-as-first-line-maintenance-treatment-for-her2-positive-metastatic-breast-cancer)
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Pfizer’s Tukysa (tucatinib) in combination with trastuzumab and pertuzumab as a maintenance therapy for HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer [13] [24] [97]. Separately, Novartis received FDA approval for Rhapsido (remibrutinib) as the first treatment for symptomatic dermographism, a chronic skin condition [23] [32] [102].
**What it means:** These approvals expand treatment options for patients with significant unmet needs. Pfizer's Tukysa approval reinforces its presence in the competitive HER2-positive breast cancer landscape, while Novartis' Rhapsido marks a new therapeutic class for symptomatic dermographism [110] [192].
