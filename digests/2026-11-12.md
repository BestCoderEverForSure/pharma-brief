## Top Stories

### 1. [FDA Orders New Vision-Loss Warning for GLP-1 Drugs](https://kmph.com/news/nation-world/fda-orders-new-warning-labels-for-glp-1-drugs-over-rare-vision-loss-risk-ozempic-wegovy-zepbound-diabetes-weight-loss-side-effects-non-arteritic-anterior-ischemic-optic-neuropathy-naion)
**What happened:** The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has mandated new warning labels for GLP-1 receptor agonist drugs, including popular GLP-1 medications, such as Ozempic, to include a rare risk of vision loss, specifically non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) [2] [25] [45] [98].
**What it means:** This new safety concern, though rare, adds to the growing list of potential side effects for the blockbuster GLP-1 class. It will require prescribers to counsel patients on this risk and could lead to increased scrutiny and potential litigation, impacting patient confidence and prescribing patterns in the highly competitive obesity and diabetes markets.

### 2. [US MFN Policy Reshapes Global Drug Launches](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/11/most-favored-nations-policy-makes-drugmakers-rethink-launches-abroad/)
**What happened:** Pharmaceutical companies are reportedly adjusting their strategies for launching new drugs internationally, particularly in Europe, to mitigate the impact of the Trump administration's Most-Favored Nations (MFN) policy, which ties US drug prices to lower prices paid in other countries [3] [5]. This development coincides with reports that drug price hikes in Germany could also push US costs higher [1].
**What it means:** The MFN policy is creating a complex global pricing environment, potentially leading to fewer new drug launches in countries with lower prices as companies prioritize protecting higher US revenues. This could limit patient access to new therapies in some markets and, paradoxically, contribute to higher drug costs in the US if companies seek to offset lower international prices. Incoming Democratic congressional chairs are also investigating these MFN agreements [5].

### 3. [FDA Shelves Speedy Drug Review Voucher Program](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/us-fda-shelves-commissioners-voucher-program-speedy-drug-reviews-sources-say-2026-11-12/)
**What happened:** The US FDA has reportedly shelved its commissioner's voucher program, which allowed pharmaceutical companies to pay for expedited drug reviews [13] [46]. This decision follows BioNTech's recent payment of $205 million for such a voucher to accelerate its oncology pipeline [33].
**What it means:** This move removes a controversial pathway for faster regulatory approvals, potentially slowing down the review process for some innovative therapies, particularly in areas like oncology where companies were leveraging these vouchers for strategic advantage. It signals a shift in the FDA's approach to incentivizing and managing drug development timelines.
