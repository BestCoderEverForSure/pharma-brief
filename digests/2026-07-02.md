## Top Stories

### 1. [FDA widens Vertex’s Casgevy use for paediatric patients](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/us-fda-approves-vertexs-gene-therapy-sickle-cell-disease-children-young-two-2026-07-01/) {major}
**What happened:** The US FDA has approved an expanded label for Vertex Pharmaceuticals' gene therapy, Casgevy, to include children as young as two years old with sickle cell disease or transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia [8] [50] [90]. This marks Casgevy as the first gene therapy available for these rare blood disorders in this younger pediatric population [52] [61].
**What it means:** This expansion significantly broadens the eligible patient pool for Casgevy, offering a potentially curative treatment option to very young children who previously had limited choices. It reinforces the growing acceptance and regulatory pathway for gene therapies, particularly for severe genetic conditions, and solidifies Vertex's leadership in this innovative space.
**Lenses:** *Commercial* - Expands market access to a critical, underserved pediatric population. · *Scientific* - Validates the safety and efficacy of gene editing technology in younger patients. · *Regulatory* - Sets a precedent for future gene therapy approvals in early childhood.

### 2. [Ipsen to buy Swiss biotech Memo Therapeutics in potential $800M deal](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/ipsen-memo-therapeutics-acquisition-transplant-virus-800m/719859/)
**What happened:** French pharmaceutical company Ipsen has announced its intention to acquire Swiss biotech Memo Therapeutics for a potential total consideration of $800 million [1] [142]. The acquisition will provide Ipsen with Memo Therapeutics' lead experimental medicine, which is in late-stage testing for a virus that commonly affects kidney transplant patients [1].
**What it means:** This deal represents a strategic move for Ipsen to bolster its pipeline with a late-stage asset that addresses a specific, high-need patient population in transplantation. For Memo Therapeutics, it provides a substantial return and a clear path for its drug to market, while highlighting the continued M&A activity in biotech for assets with clear clinical utility.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Ipsen gains a complementary asset, reducing pipeline risk. · *Financial* - The $800M valuation reflects the potential of a late-stage asset in a niche market.

### 3. [Insilico Medicine, Takeda partner on AI-driven drug discovery](https://www.wsj.com/articles/takeda-insilico-strike-ai-drug-discovery-deal-worth-up-to-600-million-1719916920)
**What happened:** Insilico Medicine has entered a strategic collaboration with Takeda, leveraging its AI-based Pharma.AI platform for the discovery and development of drug candidates [9]. The deal could be worth up to $600 million in upfront and milestone payments [121] [141] [191]. This follows other AI-driven drug discovery partnerships, including Mankind Pharma's collaboration with Denovo Sciences [215] [220] [222] [231] [238].
**What it means:** This significant partnership underscores the pharmaceutical industry's accelerating adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) to enhance and speed up drug discovery processes. For Takeda, it's a move to integrate cutting-edge technology to potentially identify novel drug candidates more efficiently. For Insilico, it validates its AI platform and provides substantial funding for further development.
**Lenses:** *Technology* - Demonstrates the increasing role of AI platforms in early-stage drug discovery. · *Strategic* - Pharma companies are actively seeking external AI expertise to augment internal R&D capabilities.
