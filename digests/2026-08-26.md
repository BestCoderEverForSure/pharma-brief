## Top Stories
> The 2-4 stories worth real attention. Use only the lenses that apply.

### 1. Jazz Pharma's Ziihera Gains Broad FDA Approval for HER2+ Gastroesophageal Cancer {major}
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Jazz Pharmaceuticals' bispecific antibody, Ziihera (zanidatamab-hrii), as an initial treatment for all HER2-positive gastroesophageal adenocarcinomas (a type of stomach and esophageal cancer), both with and without tislelizumab plus chemotherapy [1] [20].
**What it means:** This dramatically expanded label positions Ziihera for potential blockbuster sales, offering a new first-line therapeutic option for patients with this aggressive cancer type and significantly increasing Jazz's oncology market presence [1] [21] [63].
**Lenses:** *Commercial* - Broadened indication greatly expands the addressable patient population. *Strategic* - Establishes Ziihera as a key asset in the competitive HER2+ oncology landscape.

### 2. J&J's Imaavy Approved as First Treatment for Rare Blood Disorder
**What happened:** Johnson & Johnson's Imaavy (nipocalimab-aahu) has received FDA approval as the first treatment for warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (wAIHA) (a rare blood disorder where the immune system attacks red blood cells) in patients aged 12 and older [10] [73].
**What it means:** This approval addresses a significant unmet medical need for wAIHA patients, who previously lacked targeted treatment options. J&J is aiming for Imaavy to achieve $5 billion in peak sales, highlighting the commercial potential of first-in-class therapies for rare diseases [10] [83].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - First-in-class approval for a rare disease. *Commercial* - High sales forecast for a specialized market.

### 3. Roche and Eli Lilly Secure FDA Clearance for Alzheimer's Blood Test
**What happened:** The FDA has cleared a new blood test for Alzheimer's disease, developed by Roche and Eli Lilly, for use in primary and specialty care settings. The test had previously secured CE mark certification in Europe [2] [28].
**What it means:** This marks the first single-biomarker blood test for Alzheimer's, offering a less invasive and more accessible diagnostic tool compared to traditional methods. It could significantly improve early detection, facilitate patient identification for clinical trials, and potentially accelerate the uptake of new Alzheimer's treatments [2] [28] [57] [89].
**Lenses:** *Scientific* - Advances diagnostic capabilities with a single-biomarker approach. *Commercial* - Enhances market access for Alzheimer's diagnostics and downstream therapies.
