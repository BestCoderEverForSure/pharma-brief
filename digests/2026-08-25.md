## Top Stories

### 1. [Roche, Eli Lilly's Alzheimer's Blood Test Gets FDA Clearance](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/roche-eli-lillys-alzheimers-blood-test-gets-fda-clearance-2026-08-24/) {major}
**What happened:** The FDA has granted clearance to Roche's Elecsys pTau217 blood test, co-developed with Eli Lilly, making it the first single-biomarker blood test approved for assessing Alzheimer's disease pathology in both primary and specialty care settings [22] [30] [39]. This test measures phosphorylated tau 217 (pTau217), a key biomarker for Alzheimer's [39].
**What it means:** This approval marks a significant step towards earlier and less invasive Alzheimer's diagnosis, potentially reducing reliance on costly and invasive PET scans or spinal taps [44]. For drug developers, it could streamline patient recruitment for clinical trials and enable earlier intervention with emerging treatments, fundamentally changing how the disease is managed and accelerating the development of new therapies [44].

### 2. [Roche Commits to Lean-Mass Preservation with Hanmi Obesity Deal](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/roche-pens-25b-pact-hanmis-clinical-stage-obesity-drug-targeting-less-explored-mechanism)
**What happened:** Roche has entered a licensing agreement with Hanmi Pharmaceutical for HM17321, a clinical-stage obesity drug candidate, with an upfront payment of $190 million and potential milestones up to $2.3 billion [5] [137]. The drug is an amylin analog (a deep dive into amylin analogs is available [12] [169]) designed to promote weight loss while preserving lean muscle mass, a key differentiator from current GLP-1 agonists [5].
**What it means:** This deal signals Roche's strategic entry into the lucrative obesity market with a differentiated asset, aiming to address a significant unmet need for treatments that mitigate muscle loss associated with weight reduction [13] [266]. It intensifies competition in the metabolic disease space, potentially pushing other players to explore similar muscle-sparing mechanisms.

### 3. [Safety Concerns Spur FDA to Again Halt Testing of Regenxbio Gene Therapy](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/regenxbio-gene-therapy-hunter-syndrome-clinical-hold-rgx-121-fda/691605/)
**What happened:** The FDA has placed a second clinical hold this year on Regenxbio's gene therapy, RGX-121, for Hunter syndrome (Mucopolysaccharidosis Type II) [2] [50]. The hold was triggered by the detection of masses on the spines of five patients who received the treatment, delaying the planned Biologics License Application (BLA) resubmission [35] [97].
**What it means:** This repeated clinical hold highlights the ongoing safety challenges in gene therapy, particularly for systemic administration. It raises concerns for Regenxbio's pipeline and could lead to significant delays and increased development costs, impacting investor confidence in the program and potentially in the broader gene therapy sector for rare diseases [43].

## Eli Lilly Spotlight
- **Alzheimer's Diagnostic Milestone:** Eli Lilly co-developed the newly FDA-cleared Elecsys pTau217 blood test with Roche, which will enable earlier and more accessible Alzheimer's diagnosis, and could potentially aid patient identification for Alzheimer's drug development [22] [30].
- **Foundayo UK Launch:** Lilly has launched its oral weight-loss drug, Foundayo (orforglipron), in the UK, expanding its presence in the global obesity market beyond tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) [229].
- **Retatrutide Delay Concerns:** An amino acid dispute with the FDA could potentially delay the availability of cheaper retatrutide by years, impacting Lilly's long-term strategy for its next-generation obesity and diabetes pipeline [106].
