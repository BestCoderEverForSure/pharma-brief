## Top Stories

### 1. ADA 2026: the obesity field crowds in behind Lilly and Novo
**What happened:** At the American Diabetes Association Scientific Sessions (New Orleans, 5–8 Jun), Lilly detailed Phase 3 ACHIEVE data for oral orforglipron (Foundayo) — superior to oral semaglutide on A1C and ~73.6% more relative weight loss at 52 weeks — while Novo published full CagriSema REIMAGINE Phase 3 results in *The Lancet*, and AstraZeneca said its oral GLP-1 elecoglipron is advancing to a "sprawling" Phase 3 program.
**What it means:** The headline efficacy race (injectable tirzepatide vs. CagriSema vs. retatrutide) is maturing; the strategic action is shifting to **oral** convenience, scalability and manufacturing. CagriSema previously missed non-inferiority vs. tirzepatide on weight, and Novo's own CSO called the retatrutide comparison "jury still out" — leaving Lilly with the data edge but a widening field of challengers (AZ, plus China-sourced peptides).
**Lenses:** *Scientific* — head-to-head oral superiority is a high bar Lilly cleared. *Strategic* — orals reset the moat from potency to supply/access. *Commercial* — pills expand the treatable pool but intensify the payer-coverage fight.

### 2. Supreme Court backs generics on "skinny labels"
**What happened:** On 4 Jun a unanimous Court (Justice Jackson) reversed the Federal Circuit in *Hikma v. Amarin*, holding that a generic that properly carves out a patented indication isn't liable for induced infringement. *(Dated 4 Jun — just before the window, but it defined the week.)*
**What it means:** This re-affirms the section viii "skinny label" pathway that lets generics launch on off-patent uses while carving out protected ones — a win for lower prices and a real constraint on how branded firms defend secondary-indication patents.
**Lenses:** *Regulatory/legal* — inducement now turns on the generic's own affirmative conduct, a higher bar for branded plaintiffs. *Commercial* — faster generic entry pressures branded pricing on multi-indication drugs. *Strategic* — lifecycle-management playbooks built on new-indication patents just got weaker.

### 3. Incyte adds a bleeding-disorder asset in a $2B bolt-on
**What happened:** Incyte agreed to acquire Vega Therapeutics (a Star Therapeutics subsidiary) for $1.25B upfront, up to $2B with milestones (8 Jun), gaining VGA039 — a Phase 3 anti–Protein S antibody for von Willebrand disease.
**What it means:** Another classic 2026 bolt-on: mid-size pharma buying de-risked late-stage assets to diversify. Biotech M&A had already hit ~$106B by early June, on pace for the strongest year since 2019, with average deal sizes climbing.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* — diversifies Incyte beyond its Jakafi-dependent hematology base. *Financial* — ~$1.25B R&D charge, but a Phase 3 asset with first-in-class subcutaneous potential. *Scientific* — convenient prophylaxis could displace IV infusions in VWD.
