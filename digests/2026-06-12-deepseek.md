## Top Stories

### 1. Takeda's zasocitinib tops Bristol Myers' Sotyktu in head-to-head psoriasis test
**What happened:** Takeda's oral TYK2 inhibitor zasocitinib (TAK-279) beat BMS's Sotyktu in a direct comparative trial for plaque psoriasis, supporting Takeda's long-held belief in the drug's superiority [8].
**What it means:** If replicated and eventually approved (zasocitinib is in Phase 3), this gives Takeda a clear differentiation narrative against a $1B+ oral psoriasis market incumbent. Sotyktu has had a slow launch; a better-in-class competitor could reshape the oral psoriasis landscape and justify Takeda's $4B bet on the asset.
**Lenses:** *Financial* — a win here helps justify the $4B upfront cost and creates meaningful revenue optionality post‑2027 patent cliffs · *Strategic* — Takeda needed a blockbuster pipeline candidate; zasocitinib now has a stronger commercial narrative · *Scientific* — head-to-head superiority is a high bar; replicating this in a pivotal setting will be key.

### 2. Novartis RNA drug del-brax notches a Phase 1/2 win, validating $12B Avidity deal
**What happened:** Del-brax, an "antisense oligonucleotide conjugate" for a muscle-wasting disease that Novartis acquired in its $12B purchase of Avidity Biosciences, met its primary endpoint in a Phase 1/2 study [7].
**What it means:** The data provide early proof that the Avidity platform works — critical for Novartis, which paid a huge premium (78% share premium) for access to the antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate technology. A positive readout in a rare neuromuscular disease de-risks the broader platform and the rest of Avidity's pipeline now inside Novartis.
**Lenses:** *Financial* — first pipeline validation since the blockbuster deal; protects the acquisition's ROI thesis · *Strategic* — Novartis is betting on RNA therapeutics as a new pillar alongside molecular glues [2] · *Scientific* — Phase 1/2 success in a tough indication (muscle wasting) is encouraging for the platform's broader potential.

### 3. Employer coverage of obesity drugs is shrinking — and that's a big deal
**What happened:** More U.S. employers are planning to drop coverage of GLP-1 drugs for weight loss next year, as the number of people taking the medications continues to climb [3].
**What it means:** The GLP-1 boom has been fuelled by broad insurance coverage, but employers are balking at the cost. If this trend accelerates, it could cap the addressable patient pool for tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), semaglutide, and the incoming wave of oral GLP-1s. Market access, not just efficacy, is becoming the bottleneck.
**Lenses:** *Commercial & market access* — payer/formulary dynamics are tightening faster than many expected · *Financial* — a non-trivial headwind for revenue forecasts across the entire obesity class · *Geopolitical & macro* — self-funded employer plans have more flexibility to drop coverage, unlike Medicare (which cannot cover weight loss alone); this creates a bifurcated market.
