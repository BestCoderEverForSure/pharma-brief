## Top Stories

### 1. Novo Nordisk Licenses Chinese Oral Obesity Pill for up to $2.6 Billion {major}
**What happened:** Novo Nordisk has agreed to pay China's Hengrui Pharma up to $2.6 billion for an exclusive license agreement for HRS-1596, an investigational once-weekly oral GLP-1 receptor agonist for obesity [2] [16] [238].
**What it means:** This significant deal provides Novo Nordisk with a new oral GLP-1 asset to compete with Eli Lilly's market-leading Zepbound and its own oral semaglutide, potentially calming investor concerns about its future pipeline in the rapidly expanding obesity market [2] [11] [16].
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Bolsters Novo's oral obesity pipeline and competitive stance against Lilly. · *Financial* - Large upfront and milestone payments reflect high value placed on next-gen obesity assets. · *Geopolitical* - Major licensing deal with a Chinese biotech highlights China's growing role in drug discovery.

### 2. AstraZeneca Invests $2 Billion in Summit Therapeutics for Oncology Partnership
**What happened:** AstraZeneca is making a $2 billion equity investment in Summit Therapeutics and partnering to test Summit's ivonescimab, a PD-1/VEGF bispecific antibody, alongside AstraZeneca's own oncology drugs [5] [10]. Separately, Merck (MSD) has committed up to $2.13 billion for a KRAS inhibitor from SciBrunch [17] [162].
**What it means:** These deals signal continued big pharma appetite for innovative oncology assets, allowing AstraZeneca to explore combination therapies and Merck to target a historically challenging pathway. They highlight a strategic focus on expanding oncology portfolios through partnerships and targeted acquisitions [5] [10] [17] [162].
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Expands AstraZeneca's and Merck's oncology pipelines and combination therapy options. · *Financial* - Multi-billion dollar commitments demonstrate confidence in these assets and market potential. · *Scientific* - Focus on bispecifics and KRAS inhibitors reflects evolving oncology treatment paradigms.

### 3. Trump Administration's Pharmaceutical Tariffs Take Effect
**What happened:** The Trump administration's executive order imposing a 100% tariff on certain imported pharmaceutical drugs has officially taken effect, with the US detailing rules for specialty pharmaceutical tariff exemptions [36] [25] [79]. Lawmakers are urging the administration to reconsider a related rule that could restrict imported medicines from Canada and overseas [7].
**What it means:** This policy aims to incentivize domestic drug manufacturing but could lead to increased drug costs for consumers and supply chain disruptions, prompting concerns from lawmakers and potentially impacting the availability of medicines from international pharmacies [7] [36].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory/Geopolitical* - Represents a significant shift in US trade policy impacting the pharmaceutical sector. · *Commercial* - Could drive up drug prices and alter supply chain strategies for companies operating in the US.
