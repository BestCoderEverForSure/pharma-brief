## Top Stories

### 1. [US Regulators Intensify Drug Pricing and Access Initiatives](https://statnews.com/2026/06/15/fda-approves-colorados-plan-to-import-cheaper-drugs-from-canada/) {major}
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Colorado's plan to import certain prescription drugs from Canada, aiming to lower prices for residents [6], [22]. Concurrently, a proposed federal rule for 2029 seeks to close a "loophole" that protects medicines transitioning from intravenous (IV) to under-the-skin (subcutaneous) administration from Medicare price negotiation, potentially impacting blockbusters like Merck's Keytruda and Bristol Myers Squibb's Opdivo [1]. Separately, a temporary Medicare program will offer GLP-1 weight-loss drugs for $50 starting July 1, a move analysts suggest may be difficult to reverse [7].
**What it means:** These actions represent a significant escalation in US efforts to control drug costs and expand patient access. Colorado's import plan sets a precedent for other states, potentially fragmenting the US drug market and creating new supply chain complexities. The proposed rule for IV-to-subcutaneous transitions directly targets a common strategy used by pharma to extend patent exclusivity and maintain high prices, forcing companies to reconsider lifecycle management for key assets. The temporary GLP-1 program, while intended to be short-term, could establish a permanent pathway for Medicare coverage of obesity drugs, a major win for patients but a potential long-term cost burden for the system.
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - The FDA's approval of Colorado's import plan is a landmark decision, challenging traditional drug distribution models. *Commercial & Market Access* - Pharma companies face increased pressure on pricing and reimbursement, particularly for high-cost oncology and metabolic therapies. *Strategic* - Companies with large injectable portfolios will need to re-evaluate their R&D and commercial strategies to mitigate future price negotiation risks.

### 2. [Merck and AstraZeneca Secure Key Oncology Approvals](https://pharmexec.com/view/fda-approves-two-indications-of-keytruda-in-combination-with-welireg-pharmexec)
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Merck's Keytruda (pembrolizumab) and Keytruda Qlex, in combination with Welireg (belzutifan), as an adjuvant treatment for adults with renal clear cell carcinoma (ccRCC) after surgery [19], [28], [40], [41], [83], [87], [105]. Separately, AstraZeneca's Truqap (capivasertib) received FDA approval in combination with abiraterone and prednisone for PTEN-deficient prostate cancer [68], [117].
**What it means:** These approvals expand treatment options for two significant cancer types. The Keytruda/Welireg combination provides a new adjuvant therapy for high-risk kidney cancer, potentially improving outcomes for patients post-surgery. This further solidifies Keytruda's dominance across multiple oncology indications. AstraZeneca's Truqap approval targets a specific genetic subtype of prostate cancer (PTEN-deficient), offering a precision medicine approach for patients who often have aggressive disease. These developments underscore the continued advancement in oncology, particularly in combination therapies and biomarker-driven treatments.
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - The approvals highlight the efficacy of combination immunotherapies and targeted agents in improving patient outcomes. *Strategic & Competitive* - Merck strengthens its oncology franchise with another Keytruda-based approval, while AstraZeneca gains ground in prostate cancer with a targeted therapy. *Commercial & Market Access* - These new indications will expand the addressable patient populations for both drugs, driving revenue growth, but will also face scrutiny under the evolving drug pricing landscape.

## Eli Lilly Spotlight
- **JAK Inhibitor Data:** Eli Lilly's acquisition of Ajax appears to be paying off, with early positive data for a new JAK drug showing encouraging effects in patients with hard-to-treat myelofibrosis [2], [10], [53]. This could position Lilly as a competitor to Incyte's Jakafi (ruxolitinib) in this indication.
- **Mounjaro's Market Leadership:** Lilly's Mounjaro (tirzepatide) continues to hold the number one position among leading pharma brands in India, demonstrating strong commercial performance in a key emerging market [201].
- **Manufacturing Expansion:** Lilly's decision to locate its new $3.5 billion manufacturing facility in Pennsylvania underscores its commitment to expanding US production capacity, potentially influenced by looming tariff threats [112].
- **Competitive Landscape:** Neumora, after a setback in depression, is reportedly shifting its focus towards Alzheimer's and obesity drugs, indicating a growing competitive field in areas where Lilly has significant interests [88].

## On the radar
- **Sanofi's Tzield Expansion:** The FDA has expanded the use of Sanofi's Tzield (teplizumab) for Type 1 Diabetes to include children, broadening its market reach [62], [63], [77].
- **OTC CGM for Children:** The FDA cleared Dexcom's Stelo for over-the-counter (OTC) continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) in children, expanding access to this technology for pediatric patients [65], [69], [70], [73].
- **Neumora Layoffs & Pipeline Shift:** Neumora will lay off 35% of its staff after its depression candidate, navacaprant, failed in late-stage studies, leading the company to pivot towards Alzheimer's and obesity drugs [81], [88], [194].
- **Aurobindo Pharma's USFDA Flag:** Aurobindo Pharma's Eugia Unit-III manufacturing facility has been classified as Official Action Indicated (OAI) by the USFDA, leading to a drop in its share price [197], [211], [212], [231], [252].
- **Pfizer's Penicillin Shortage:** Amid a national shortage of penicillin, Pfizer has not fully complied with requests to donate emergency supplies to state health agencies, contributing to a public health crisis around congenital syphilis [11], [12].
- **Jazz Pharma's Zepzelca Failure:** Jazz Pharmaceuticals' lung cancer drug Zepzelca (lurbinectedin) failed a Phase III trial in relapsed small-cell lung cancer, raising regulatory uncertainty [160], [203].
- **Traws Pharma Flu Trial Delay:** Traws Pharma's mid-phase flu study has been deferred after a negative regulatory review by the UK regulator, causing its stock to tumble [109], [216], [219], [222], [226], [234].
- **J&J Acquires Firefly Bio:** Johnson & Johnson is set to acquire Firefly Bio for $1 billion, enhancing its oncology pipeline [193].
