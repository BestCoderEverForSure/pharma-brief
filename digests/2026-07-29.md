## Top Stories

### 1. [GSK unveils $2.5B cost-cutting plan amid patent cliff](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/gsk-patent-cliff-cost-cutting-plan-25-billion/721867/)
**What happened:** GSK's new CEO Luke Miels outlined a strategy to cut $2.5 billion in costs, primarily from mature products, procurement, and supply chain, to offset the impending loss of patent exclusivity for its HIV drug franchise [1] [4] [198]. The company also plans to pursue more late-stage drug trials [4].
**What it means:** This move signals a significant strategic pivot for GSK, aiming to streamline operations and reallocate resources towards its pipeline to secure long-term growth as key revenue drivers face generic competition. It reflects a broader industry trend of large pharmaceutical companies proactively managing patent expirations.
**Lenses:** *Financial* - The $2.5 billion in cost savings is intended to absorb the financial impact of patent losses, protecting margins. *Strategic* - The plan aims to re-position GSK for future growth by focusing on late-stage R&D and operational efficiency.

### 2. [Argenx to acquire Forte Biosciences for $2.2bn](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/argenx-forte-biosciences-acquisition/)
**What happened:** Argenx has entered a definitive agreement to acquire Forte Biosciences for approximately $2.2 billion in total equity value [10]. This acquisition will integrate Forte Biosciences into Argenx's portfolio.
**What it means:** This substantial acquisition highlights continued M&A activity in the biotech sector, with larger companies seeking to bolster their pipelines and expand therapeutic areas. For Forte Biosciences, it represents a significant return for investors, including Biotech Growth Trust which saw a 14-times return [108] [174].
**Lenses:** *Financial* - The $2.2 billion deal value represents a major investment and potential growth driver for Argenx, while delivering a significant payout for Forte Biosciences shareholders. *Strategic* - The acquisition expands Argenx's portfolio, potentially adding new assets or capabilities in areas of interest.

### 3. [FDA staff cast doubt on Replimune's melanoma drug's benefit; shares slide](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/us-fda-staff-cast-doubt-replimune-cancer-drugs-benefit-shares-slide-2026-07-28/)
**What happened:** FDA staff reiterated concerns about Replimune's skin cancer treatment (RP1), stating that the Phase 3 trial's data package was "not interpretable" and did not meet its objectives [12] [14] [22]. This assessment led to a significant drop in Replimune's shares [14] [76] [88].
**What it means:** This FDA feedback creates substantial uncertainty for Replimune's lead candidate, potentially delaying or jeopardizing its approval. It underscores the rigorous scrutiny applied to clinical trial data, especially for novel cancer therapies, and highlights the challenges biotechs face in meeting regulatory efficacy standards.
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - The FDA's strong concerns about data interpretability signal a difficult path forward for approval, likely requiring further studies or clarification. *Financial* - Replimune's stock plunged, reflecting investor apprehension about the drug's commercial prospects and the company's future valuation.
