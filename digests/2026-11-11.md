## Top Stories

### 1. FDA Issues New GLP-1 Warning for Vision Loss {major}
**What happened:** The FDA is requiring new warning labels for GLP-1 receptor agonists, including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound, to include a potential risk of a rare but serious eye condition, sometimes referred to as an "eye stroke," which can cause vision loss [5] [58] [97].
**What it means:** This safety update affects a major class of blockbuster drugs used for diabetes and weight loss, potentially impacting patient perception and prescribing patterns, even if the risk is rare. It underscores the FDA's ongoing post-market surveillance for widely used medications [5] [96].
**Lenses:** *Commercial* - Potential, though likely limited, impact on patient confidence and prescribing given the rarity of the event. *Regulatory* - Demonstrates the FDA's proactive safety monitoring and willingness to update labels for high-volume drugs.

### 2. Beam Therapeutics Achieves Pivotal Success for Sickle Cell Gene Therapy
**What happened:** Beam Therapeutics announced its base editing therapy for sickle cell disease met its primary endpoint in a pivotal trial, positioning the company for an FDA application as early as the end of the year [2] [48] [102].
**What it means:** This marks a scientific milestone as a first-of-its-kind base editing therapy, potentially offering a new genetic treatment option for sickle cell disease. It sets up a competitive dynamic with existing gene therapies like Casgevy and Lyfgenia [2] [102] [151].
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - Validation of base editing technology for a severe genetic disorder. *Strategic & Competitive* - Positions Beam as a contender in the gene therapy space, challenging established treatments.

### 3. Bayer's Asundexian Approved by FDA for Stroke Prevention
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Asundexian (Sunharbo), Bayer's first-in-class Factor XIa (FXIa) inhibitor, for reducing the recurrence of stroke [6] [43] [51] [65].
**What it means:** This approval introduces a novel mechanism of action for secondary stroke prevention, offering a new option for patients at risk. It could carve out a significant market share in the cardiovascular space, which has seen limited innovation in this specific area [43] [50] [105].
**Lenses:** *Commercial & Market Access* - New treatment option for a large patient population, potentially impacting existing anticoagulant markets. *Scientific & Clinical* - Validation of FXIa inhibition as an effective strategy for stroke prevention.
