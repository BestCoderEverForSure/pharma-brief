## Top Stories

### 1. FDA Places Partial Hold on Biohaven’s Epilepsy Drug Opakalim
**What happened:** The FDA has placed a partial clinical hold on Biohaven’s epilepsy drug candidate, opakalim, halting new patient enrollment in ongoing trials. This decision comes just weeks after Biohaven licensed global rights to the therapy to SK Biopharmaceuticals in a deal potentially worth up to $350 million [1] [17] [18] [73].
**What it means:** This regulatory setback introduces uncertainty for opakalim's development and could impact future milestone payments for Biohaven. For SK Biopharmaceuticals, it means a delay in advancing a key asset and potentially facing a deferred $400 million payment that was contingent on FDA resolution [70] [73] [97]. The hold underscores the inherent regulatory risks in drug development, even for assets considered promising enough for significant licensing deals.
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - The partial hold could delay pivotal trial completion and potential approval. *Financial* - The $400 million payment from SK Biopharmaceuticals is now awaiting FDA hold resolution, creating financial uncertainty for Biohaven. *Strategic* - SK Biopharmaceuticals' acquisition strategy is immediately challenged by this unexpected regulatory hurdle.

### 2. Bayer’s Hyrnuo Receives Expanded FDA Approval for First-Line Lung Cancer
**What happened:** The FDA has granted accelerated approval to Bayer’s Hyrnuo (sevabertinib) for the first-line treatment of adult patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring HER2-activating mutations [5] [11] [28] [54] [67].
**What it means:** This accelerated approval positions Hyrnuo as a front-line option, significantly broadening its market potential and competitive standing against other lung cancer therapies. It offers a new targeted treatment for a specific patient population, reinforcing Bayer's oncology portfolio.
**Lenses:** *Commercial* - Moving to first-line treatment dramatically increases the addressable patient population and sales potential. *Strategic* - Strengthens Bayer's presence in the competitive lung cancer market, offering a differentiated therapy.

### 3. Takeda’s R&D Chief Andy Plump to Retire Next Year
**What happened:** Andy Plump, Takeda’s President of Research and Development and a key figure in the company’s R&D transformation, has announced his retirement effective next year [2]. Plump joined Takeda during a period of significant change and is credited with fostering closer collaboration with emerging biotechs.
**What it means:** Plump's departure marks a significant leadership transition for Takeda, particularly in its critical R&D function. His successor will inherit the task of continuing the company's innovation strategy and maintaining its pipeline momentum, especially given Takeda's recent focus on external partnerships and biotech collaborations.
**Lenses:** *Leadership* - Signals a major change in Takeda's executive leadership, potentially influencing future R&D direction. *Strategic* - The new R&D head will need to sustain the "lasting foundation" of innovation and biotech collaboration that Plump established.
