### 2. [Labor Department Finalizes Sweeping PBM Disclosure Rule](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/29/labor-department-finalizes-sweeping-pbm-disclosure-rule/)
**What happened:** The US Labor Department has finalized a new rule requiring pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) to disclose a broad range of fee and compensation data to their self-funded employer clients, effective 2027 [2].
**What it means:** This rule aims to increase transparency in the opaque drug supply chain, potentially empowering employers to negotiate better deals and putting pressure on PBM business models. The move is cheered by employers but criticized by PBMs as government overreach [2].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory & Legal* - Marks a significant regulatory intervention into PBM operations, reflecting ongoing scrutiny of drug pricing. · *Commercial & Market Access* - Could shift leverage in drug purchasing, impacting PBM profitability and potentially leading to changes in how drugs are reimbursed and accessed.

### 3. [HHS Green Lights Six Peptides, Despite Concerns From FDA Scientists](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/29/hhs-green-lights-six-peptides-despite-concerns-from-fda-scientists/)
**What happened:** The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), under RFK Jr., announced it would not take enforcement action against six unapproved peptides, temporarily allowing compounding pharmacies to manufacture them for wellness-focused compounded medicines [4] [7] [9]. This decision was made despite objections from career scientists at the FDA [7] [42].
**What it means:** This controversial policy bypasses the traditional FDA approval process, raising significant concerns about drug safety and regulatory integrity. It highlights a growing tension between political directives and scientific consensus in public health policy, potentially setting a precedent for other unapproved substances [6] [53].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory & Legal* - The move represents a direct challenge to the FDA's authority and process for ensuring drug safety and efficacy. · *Geopolitical & Macro* - Reflects political influence on health policy, potentially impacting public trust in regulatory bodies and opening the door to broader access to unproven treatments.
