## Top Stories

### 1. [Vertex acquires Crinetics Pharmaceuticals for $10 billion as biotech M&A booms](https://statnews.com/2026/07/06/vertex-acquires-crinetics-pharmaceuticals-for-10-billion-as-biotech-m-a-booms/) {major}
**What happened:** Vertex Pharmaceuticals announced its acquisition of Crinetics Pharmaceuticals for approximately $10 billion, or $8.8 billion net of estimated cash acquired [5] [11]. Crinetics' lead drug is for a rare endocrine disorder [5].
**What it means:** This acquisition significantly strengthens Vertex's rare disease pipeline, following its recent success with Casgevy. It highlights big pharma's appetite for late-stage or approved assets in specialized, high-value markets, especially as biotech M&A booms [5] [40] [199].
**Lenses:** *Financial* - A $10 billion deal underscores strong valuations for companies with promising rare disease assets [5]. · *Strategic* - Vertex is deepening its focus on rare diseases, a segment known for strong pricing power and unmet needs [199].

### 2. [Novartis to acquire ADC developer in a deal worth up to $1.5B](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/novartis-adc-acquisition-myricx-bio-cancer-drug-development/720078/)
**What happened:** Novartis is set to acquire UK-based Myricx Bio for up to $1.5 billion, including an upfront payment of $1.1 billion [2] [7] [77]. The deal brings two antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) with a novel mechanism of action targeting cell growth, marking Novartis' entry into ADC development [2] [7].
**What it means:** This move positions Novartis to compete in the rapidly evolving ADC market, a key area in oncology [2] [75]. The acquisition, fueled by patent cliffs, demonstrates big pharma's strategy to bolster pipelines with innovative platforms to drive future growth [121].
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Novartis gains a foothold in the competitive ADC field, diversifying its oncology portfolio [2]. · *Financial* - The deal's structure, with a significant upfront payment, reflects confidence in Myricx's ADC technology and its potential market value [7].

### 3. [FDA Expands Casgevy Approval to Children as Young as 2 Years With Sickle Cell Disease or Transfusion-Dependent β-Thalassemia](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-expands-casgevy-approval-to-children-as-young-as-2-years-with-sickle-cell-disease-or-transfusion-dependent-b-thalassemia)
**What happened:** The FDA expanded the approval of Vertex's gene-editing therapy Casgevy to include children as young as two years old with sickle cell disease or transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia [90] [110]. Additionally, the FDA approved the first treatment for thyroid eye disease [17] and Pfizer’s IBRANCE for HR+/HER2+ metastatic breast cancer [41] [88]. In a notable development for AI in drug discovery, Absentia Labs' Digital Liver Model became the first AI drug development tool accepted into the FDA Qualification Program [18].
**What it means:** The Casgevy label expansion significantly broadens access to a transformative gene therapy for younger patients, while new approvals address unmet needs in specific disease areas. The FDA's acceptance of an AI tool into its qualification program signals a growing regulatory embrace of artificial intelligence to streamline and de-risk drug development processes [18] [10] [22].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - FDA is actively expanding access to innovative therapies and is increasingly engaging with AI for drug development [18] [86]. · *Scientific* - Gene therapy continues to show promise for rare genetic disorders, and AI is proving its utility beyond early discovery into formulation and regulatory pathways [90] [10] [22].
