## Top Stories

### 1. FDA Orders New Vision Loss Warning for GLP-1 Drugs
**What happened:** The FDA has mandated label changes for all GLP-1 receptor agonists, including popular weight-loss and diabetes medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Eli Lilly's Zepbound, to include a warning about a potential risk of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION), a rare but serious eye condition sometimes referred to as an "eye stroke" [10] [17] [19] [72] [79] [95].
**What it means:** While the FDA notes the condition is rare, this class-wide warning could impact patient perception and prescribing patterns for these high-growth drugs. Companies will need to manage potential patient concerns and ensure clear communication regarding the risk-benefit profile.
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* - The FDA's action reflects ongoing post-market surveillance for widely used drugs. *Commercial* - Manufacturers will need to update marketing and patient education materials, potentially facing increased scrutiny from prescribers and patients.

### 2. Bayer's New Blood Thinner Class Gets FDA Nod
**What happened:** Bayer has received FDA approval for Sunharbo (asundexian), a first-in-class Factor XIa inhibitor, for the prevention of recurrent stroke [3] [8] [22] [115]. This marks a significant advancement in anticoagulation, offering a new mechanism to prevent blood clots.
**What it means:** Sunharbo is anticipated to be a potential blockbuster, as Factor XIa inhibitors aim to reduce the risk of stroke and other thrombotic events with a potentially lower risk of bleeding complications compared to current standard-of-care anticoagulants [3] [96]. This approval positions Bayer to challenge established players in the large and competitive stroke prevention market, offering a differentiated safety profile.
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - The approval validates a novel anticoagulant mechanism, potentially improving the benefit-risk balance for patients. *Commercial & Strategic* - This represents a major pipeline win for Bayer, providing a new growth driver in a high-value therapeutic area.

### 3. WuXi AppTec Removed from US Military List
**What happened:** The Trump administration has removed WuXi AppTec, a major Chinese contract research, development, and manufacturing organization (CRDMO), from its list of "Chinese military companies" operating in the U.S. [9] [73] [219].
**What it means:** This delisting alleviates immediate geopolitical pressure that had been building on WuXi AppTec and its numerous global pharmaceutical partners. For many pharma companies, this removes a significant compliance and operational headache, allowing them to continue leveraging WuXi AppTec's capabilities without the immediate threat of sanctions [9] [73] [219].
**Lenses:** *Geopolitical & Macro* - The decision signals a potential easing of tensions in the US-China biotech relationship, at least for this specific entity. *Strategic & Supply Chain* - For many pharma companies, this removes a significant compliance and operational headache, allowing them to continue leveraging WuXi AppTec's capabilities without the immediate threat of sanctions.

## Eli Lilly Spotlight
- **Foundayo sleep apnea data:** Eli Lilly's oral GLP-1 drug, Foundayo (orforglipron), showed positive results in two late-stage studies for alleviating sleep apnea, though it appeared less effective than Lilly's injectable Zepbound [12]. This indicates potential for Foundayo in a new indication, but with a nuanced efficacy profile compared to its injectable counterpart.
- **GLP-1 vision loss warning:** Lilly's injectable GLP-1 drugs, Zepbound (tirzepatide) and Mounjaro, are directly impacted by the FDA's new class-wide warning regarding the rare risk of NAION (vision loss) [10] [17] [19] [72] [95]. This necessitates label changes and careful communication for Lilly's key metabolic franchise.
