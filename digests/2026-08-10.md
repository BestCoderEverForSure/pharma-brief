## Top Stories

### 1. Sanofi Faces Shortages of Pompe Disease Medicines
**What happened:** Sanofi is experiencing shortages of two critical medicines used to treat Pompe disease, a rare genetic disorder, raising concerns among patient advocacy groups [1].
**What it means:** This highlights ongoing vulnerabilities in the pharmaceutical supply chain, particularly for rare disease therapies where patient populations are small and alternatives are limited. Such shortages can severely disrupt treatment regimens and patient health, underscoring the need for robust manufacturing and distribution strategies [1].
**Lenses:** *Commercial & Market Access* - Direct impact on patient access and Sanofi's ability to supply a niche market. *Regulatory & Legal* - Potential for regulatory scrutiny on supply chain resilience.

### 2. FDA Approves Oveporexton for Narcolepsy Type 1
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Oveporexton for the treatment of narcolepsy type 1 (NT1), a chronic neurological condition. Additionally, Sasineprocel received a Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy (RMAT) designation for Parkinson's Disease [2].
**What it means:** Oveporexton's approval provides a new therapeutic option for NT1 patients, addressing an unmet need in managing this debilitating sleep disorder. The RMAT designation for Sasineprocel could accelerate its development and review process, potentially bringing a new Parkinson's treatment to market faster by offering expedited review and intensive FDA guidance [2].
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - New treatment option for NT1. *Regulatory & Legal* - RMAT designation signals the FDA's commitment to expediting promising regenerative therapies.

### 3. Scholar Rock's SMA Drug BLA Advances with September Action Date
**What happened:** Scholar Rock announced that the FDA's review of its Biologics License Application (BLA) for apitegromab, a potential treatment for spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), is progressing. The FDA has set a Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) action date of September 30, 2026, with the review now including a second fill-finish facility [3].
**What it means:** This update provides clarity on the regulatory timeline for apitegromab, a crucial step towards potentially offering a new therapy for SMA patients. The inclusion of a second manufacturing facility addresses potential supply chain concerns and demonstrates readiness for commercialization, if approved, mitigating risks associated with single-source production [3].
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - Potential new treatment for a rare neurological disorder. *Regulatory & Legal* - Clear PDUFA date and manufacturing readiness are key de-risking factors for approval and launch.
