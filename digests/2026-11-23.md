### 2. FDA approves Novo Nordisk’s Sogroya for paediatric Turner syndrome
**What happened:** The US FDA has approved Novo Nordisk’s once-weekly Sogroya injection for treating growth failure in patients aged 2.5 years and older with Turner syndrome [4].
**What it means:** This approval expands Sogroya's label, allowing Novo Nordisk to address a specific pediatric population with growth hormone deficiency. It strengthens Novo's position in the growth hormone market, offering a convenient once-weekly option for patients and potentially increasing market share against existing daily therapies [4].

### 3. Drug developers fine-tune open AI models on proprietary data
**What happened:** Leading drug developers are increasingly opting to fine-tune open-source artificial intelligence (AI) models using their own proprietary datasets, rather than relying solely on off-the-shelf or fully bespoke AI solutions [1].
**What it means:** This approach allows companies to harness the broad capabilities of open models while tailoring them to specific drug discovery challenges and internal data, potentially accelerating R&D timelines and improving the accuracy of predictions. It represents a strategic shift towards hybrid AI strategies, balancing cost, flexibility, and data security [1].
