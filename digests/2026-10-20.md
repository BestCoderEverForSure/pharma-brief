## Top Stories

### 1. [Merck KGaA Acquires Silence Therapeutics in $1.3B RNAi Deal](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/merck-kgaa-buys-silence-13b-deal-after-sirna-drugs-phase-2-blood-cancer-win)
**What happened:** Germany's Merck KGaA has agreed to acquire UK-based Silence Therapeutics for $1.3 billion, gaining access to its RNA interference (RNAi) platform and a promising blood disorder medicine that successfully completed a Phase 2 trial [2] [100] [137].
**What it means:** This acquisition strengthens Merck KGaA's pipeline in a high-potential therapeutic area, positioning it to compete with existing treatments like Takeda's drug for blood disorders [2]. The deal highlights continued big pharma interest in RNAi technology, which uses small RNA molecules to silence disease-causing genes.
**Lenses:** *Financial* - The $1.3 billion deal value reflects a significant premium for Silence Therapeutics, whose stock soared 125% on the news [151]. *Strategic* - Merck KGaA gains a proprietary RNAi platform and a late-stage asset, enhancing its portfolio and competitive stance in rare blood disorders [2] [100].

### 2. [Roche Inks $15B ADC Pact with China's Hansoh Pharma](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/roche-returns-chinas-adc-powerhouse-hansoh-15b-biobucks-pact)
**What happened:** Roche has entered a broad collaboration and licensing agreement with China's Hansoh Pharma, involving multiple antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) assets, with an upfront payment of $400 million and potential total milestones exceeding $15 billion [3] [146] [201].
**What it means:** This massive deal underscores the pharmaceutical industry's strong belief in ADCs (a class of potent anti-cancer drugs that deliver chemotherapy directly to cancer cells) as a key oncology modality, and China's growing role as a source of innovative biopharma assets [10] [146]. For Roche, it expands its oncology pipeline with diverse ADC candidates.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Roche gains access to a broad portfolio of ADCs, critical for maintaining competitiveness in the rapidly evolving oncology market [3] [146]. *Geopolitical* - The deal highlights the increasing importance of Chinese biotech in global drug development, even as geopolitical tensions rise [201].

### 3. [China Moves to Restrict Foreign Biotech Acquisitions](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/19/china-moves-to-restrict-foreign-biotech-acquisitions/)
**What happened:** China is proposing new restrictions on foreign acquisitions of critical biopharma technologies, aiming to retain control over its burgeoning domestic drug development industry [8] [77].
**What it means:** This policy shift could significantly impact cross-border M&A activity, making it harder for foreign companies to acquire innovative Chinese biotech platforms and intellectual property [8] [139]. It signals China's intent to foster indigenous innovation and reduce reliance on foreign ownership in strategic sectors.
**Lenses:** *Geopolitical* - The move reflects a broader trend of technological nationalism, potentially leading to a more fragmented global biotech landscape [8] [141]. *Strategic* - Foreign companies may need to adjust their China strategies, potentially focusing more on licensing deals or local partnerships rather than outright acquisitions [141].
