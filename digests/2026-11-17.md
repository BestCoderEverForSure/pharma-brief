## Top Stories

### 1. [Roche's New Breast Cancer Pill Wilserdly Gains FDA Approval](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/16/fda-approves-new-breast-cancer-drug-providing-new-option-for-some-patients-boost-for-roche/) {major}
**What happened:** The FDA approved Roche's oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) Wilserdly (giredestrant) in combination with everolimus for patients with ESR1-mutated, ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer [6] [25] [26] [38]. Roche has indicated this drug could become its top-selling product ever [6].
**What it means:** This approval marks Roche's entry into the oral SERD market, intensifying competition with existing and pipeline therapies from rivals. Its potential as a top-selling product underscores the significant market opportunity in ESR1-mutated breast cancer, offering a new oral option for patients [6] [34] [38].

### 2. [J&J Bets Up to $3.3 Billion on Ollin Biosciences for Vision Loss](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/jj-pays-22b-upfront-ollin-challenge-roches-vabysmo-phase-3-bispecific)
**What happened:** Johnson & Johnson (J&J) agreed to acquire Ollin Biosciences for an upfront payment of $2.2 billion, with potential additional payments bringing the total deal value to up to $3.3 billion [4] [15] [77]. The acquisition centers on Ollin's lead asset, a bispecific antibody in Phase III development for wet age-related macular degeneration (wAMD) and diabetic macular edema (DME) [15] [152].
**What it means:** This strategic acquisition bolsters J&J's existing eye care franchise and positions it to compete in a crowded and lucrative retina drug market, directly challenging established therapies like Roche's Vabysmo [4] [152]. The substantial investment reflects J&J's confidence in the late-stage asset's potential to capture significant market share.

### 3. [FDA Rejects Summit Therapeutics' Lung Cancer Drug ivonescimab](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/summit-therapeutics-ivonescimab-fda-rejection-lung-cancer-shares-fall/700854/)
**What happened:** The FDA issued a rejection for Summit Therapeutics' PD-1/VEGF bispecific antibody ivonescimab for a form of lung cancer [2] [23]. The agency cited the drug's failure to initially extend overall survival in the trial, leading to a significant drop in Summit's shares [2] [84] [110].
**What it means:** This setback highlights the FDA's stringent requirements for demonstrating clear survival benefits, particularly for drugs developed in China and seeking US approval [2] [48] [104]. The decision could impact other Chinese biotechs aiming for the US market and reinforces the importance of robust clinical endpoints beyond progression-free survival for oncology approvals.
