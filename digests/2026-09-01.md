## Top Stories

### 1. Novartis, Bristol Myers Pause Autoimmune CAR-T Trials Due to Safety Concerns
**What happened:** Novartis has halted development of its autoimmune CAR-T (chimeric antigen receptor T-cell) therapy, rap-cel, across multiple indications due to observed side effects. Separately, Bristol Myers Squibb (BMS) voluntarily paused its zola-cel program, following a total of three patient deaths across the trials [1] [77].
**What it means:** This represents a significant setback for the emerging autoimmune CAR-T field, raising critical safety questions and potentially slowing the broader application of cell therapies beyond oncology. It underscores the complex risk-benefit profile of these powerful treatments.
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - Highlights severe safety challenges for CAR-T in autoimmune settings. · *Strategic & Competitive* - May cause other developers in the autoimmune CAR-T space to re-evaluate their programs.

### 2. FDA Approves Takeda and Protagonist's Mimrylo for Rare Blood Cancer
**What happened:** The FDA has approved Mimrylo (rusfertide), developed by Takeda and Protagonist Therapeutics, for the treatment of polycythemia vera (PV), a rare blood cancer [2] [31] [36] [39] [44]. This first-in-class hepcidin mimetic received a broader-than-expected label, with analysts projecting over $2 billion in annual revenue [2].
**What it means:** This approval is a major commercial victory, providing a new treatment option for PV patients and strengthening Takeda's hematology portfolio. For Protagonist, it validates their drug discovery platform and offers substantial financial upside.
**Lenses:** *Financial & Capital Markets* - Significant revenue opportunity for Takeda and Protagonist. · *Commercial & Market Access* - First-in-class status and broad label position it strongly in a rare disease market.

### 3. Eli Lilly Acquires Merida Biosciences for $2.9 Billion
**What happened:** Eli Lilly announced the acquisition of Merida Biosciences for up to $2.9 billion, aiming to bolster its immune drug pipeline with Merida's protein degradation technology targeting malfunctioning proteins in autoimmune disorders [3] [35] [109] [246].
**What it means:** This strategic acquisition demonstrates Lilly's continued focus on diversifying its portfolio beyond metabolic diseases and investing in innovative platforms. The protein degradation space is highly competitive, and this deal positions Lilly with a novel approach in autoimmune conditions.
**Lenses:** *Strategic & Competitive* - Expands Lilly's autoimmune footprint and technology base. · *Technology & Innovation* - Integrates a promising protein degradation platform into a major pharma's pipeline.
