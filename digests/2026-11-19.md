## Top Stories

### 1. [White House pulls nomination for FDA nominee Overton](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-commissioner-nomination-overton-pulled-white-house/700010/)
**What happened:** The White House withdrew Heidi Overton's nomination to lead the FDA, citing concerns she lacked sufficient Senate support for confirmation [2] [9] [24] [47]. The agency's acting commissioner will remain in charge [47].
**What it means:** This decision ensures a Democrat-controlled Congress will likely select the next FDA leader, but it prolongs leadership uncertainty at a crucial time for drug approvals and policy implementation [2] [7] [101]. The lack of a confirmed commissioner could impact the agency's long-term strategic direction and its ability to address complex issues like drug pricing and novel therapy regulation [72].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* · *Geopolitical*

### 2. [GSK’s $10.6bn Nuvalent bet pays off with second lung cancer drug approval](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/gsk-nuvalent-lung-cancer-drug/)
**What happened:** GSK received FDA approval for Nelkarmi, a lung cancer drug acquired through its $10.6 billion Nuvalent acquisition, for ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [17] [120] [208]. This marks GSK's second lung cancer drug approval this year [161]. Separately, Summit Therapeutics' ivonescimab was rejected by the FDA, with the complete response letter citing issues including China-heavy trial data and a lack of overall survival gain [4] [8] [96].
**What it means:** The Nelkarmi approval validates GSK's significant investment in Nuvalent, strengthening its oncology pipeline and positioning it to compete directly with established ALK inhibitors like Roche's Alecensa [17]. For Summit, the rejection underscores the FDA's scrutiny of clinical trial diversity and endpoints, particularly when a substantial portion of data comes from outside the US, impacting its lung cancer ambitions [8] [96].
**Lenses:** *Strategic* · *Scientific & Clinical* · *Regulatory*

### 3. [Pfizer leaps into ‘in vivo’ CAR-T with latest China deal](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/pfizer-in-vivo-car-t-china-starna-autoimmune-deal/700017/)
**What happened:** Pfizer announced a collaboration with China's Starna, potentially worth $2.6 billion, to develop in vivo CAR-T (chimeric antigen receptor T-cell) therapies for autoimmune conditions [3] [153]. This deal aims to reprogram cells directly inside the body.
**What it means:** This strategic move positions Pfizer in a highly innovative and competitive area of cell therapy, potentially offering a simpler and more accessible treatment approach compared to traditional ex vivo CAR-T therapies which require cell removal and reinfusion [3]. The substantial deal value highlights Pfizer's commitment to expanding its pipeline into advanced modalities and addressing unmet needs in autoimmune diseases, diversifying its portfolio beyond conventional drugs [3].
**Lenses:** *Strategic* · *Financial* · *Technology & Innovation*
