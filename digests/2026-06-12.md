## Top Stories

### 1. Takeda's zasocitinib tops BMS's Sotyktu in psoriasis
**What happened:** Takeda's oral TYK2 inhibitor zasocitinib beat Bristol Myers Squibb's Sotyktu in a head-to-head Phase 3 in plaque psoriasis.
**What it means:** Head-to-head wins are rare and commercially decisive — Takeda now has a clean "we beat the incumbent" story for its ~$4B bet, and BMS's oral franchise loses its differentiation just as it was meant to backfill the Revlimid/Eliquis cliff.
**Lenses:** *Commercial* — superiority data is the strongest formulary argument there is · *Strategic* — repositions Takeda in immunology, pressures BMS · *Scientific* — validates next-gen selective TYK2 over first-gen.

### 2. Employers begin dropping GLP-1 coverage for weight loss
**What happened:** A growing number of employers say they'll cut coverage of GLP-1s for weight loss next year, even as prescriptions climb.
**What it means:** The entire obesity TAM rests on *who pays*. This is the first broad payer pushback — and it bites the weight-loss indication (not diabetes) hardest, exactly where Lilly's Zepbound and Novo's Wegovy compete. It reframes the growth debate from "how big" to "how durable."
**Lenses:** *Commercial* — access, not efficacy, is now the swing factor · *Financial* — pressures the volume assumptions under both stocks · *Geopolitical/macro* — feeds the broader drug-pricing/MFN climate.

### 3. Novartis's del-brax delivers on the $12B Avidity bet
**What happened:** Del-brax, an antisense oligonucleotide-conjugate from the $12B Avidity acquisition, met its primary endpoint in a muscle-wasting disease (Phase 1/2).
**What it means:** Early but real validation of a novel RNA-delivery platform Novartis paid a premium for — a high-unmet-need rare disease with orphan economics. *(Continues the Novartis del-brax/FORTITUDE thread from earlier this week.)*
**Lenses:** *Scientific* — platform proof-of-concept · *Financial* — return on a $12B outlay · *Strategic* — deepens the specialty/rare pivot.
