## Top Stories

### 1. [Takeda's $4B TYK2 drug tops Bristol Myers' Sotyktu in head-to-head test](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/takeda-zasocitinib-sotyktu-psoriasis-trial/822500/)
**What happened:** Takeda announced that zasocitinib, an oral TYK2 inhibitor (a targeted anti-inflammatory mechanism) acquired through its $4B Nimbus deal, beat BMS's Sotyktu in a head-to-head Phase 3 psoriasis trial [4].
**What it means:** Sotyktu was the first-in-class TYK2 inhibitor; Takeda now has a superiority data point that could drive formulary positioning and physician switching. This validates the hefty deal price and opens a path to challenge BMS in a multibillion-dollar autoimmune market.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* — Takeda gains a credible challenger with differentiation. *Commercial* — Head-to-head win is powerful for payer negotiations. *Scientific* — Confirms TYK2 inhibition as a competitive mechanism worth expanding into other indications.

### 2. [Novartis RNA drug acquired in $12B Avidity deal notches a trial win](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/novartis-del-brax-avidity-trial-data/822628/)
**What happened:** Del-brax, an antisense oligonucleotide conjugate (a RNA-targeting therapy) for the muscle-wasting disease DM1, met its primary endpoint in a Phase 1/2 study [3].
**What it means:** This is the first major clinical proof-of-concept for Novartis's $12B acquisition of Avidity Biosciences. It signals that the antibody–oligonucleotide conjugate platform can deliver on tough neuromuscular targets, de-risking a large pipeline bet.
**Lenses:** *Financial* — De-risks one of Novartis's largest recent M&A moves. *Scientific* — Demonstrates a novel delivery modality for RNA therapeutics. *Strategic* — Strengthens Novartis's neuromuscular and genetic medicine franchise.

### 3. [Summit pulls $500M share sale a day after announcing it](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/summit-therapeutics-pulls-500-million-share-sale-pd1-vegf-ivonescimab/822619/)
**What happened:** Summit Therapeutics withdrew its $500M share offering citing "market conditions," just one day after announcing it. Shares have struggled to break out despite promising data for ivonescimab, a PD-1/VEGF bispecific antibody [1].
**What it means:** The abrupt reversal is unusual and suggests either weak institutional demand or that management saw a better financing path. It raises questions about near-term conviction in the asset's commercial trajectory, even as ivonescimab remains one of the most-watched oncology programs.
**Lenses:** *Financial* — Unusual move signals possible valuation or demand pressure. *Strategic* — Could slow Summit's commercial buildout for a potential Keytruda competitor.
