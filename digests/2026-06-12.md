## Top Stories

### 1. Lilly adds *another* obesity shot — licenses Hanmi's sonefpeglutide
**What happened:** Lilly took exclusive worldwide (ex-Korea) rights to **sonefpeglutide**, a Hanmi weight-loss candidate — $75M upfront, up to ~$1.19B in milestones (BD&L — *business development & licensing, i.e. a deal short of a full acquisition*).
**What it means:** Lilly already leads with Zepbound and the oral pill orforglipron; adding a differentiated molecule is portfolio insurance — it boxes out rivals and hedges which mechanism ultimately wins.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* — category dominance, not single-drug reliance · *Financial* — cheap option value vs a multi-billion acquisition · *Commercial* — more shots at the various patient segments (injectable vs oral, fast vs durable).

### 2. Takeda's TYK2 drug tops Bristol Myers head-to-head
**What happened:** Takeda's **zasocitinib** (a *TYK2 inhibitor — an oral drug that dampens inflammatory signaling*) beat BMS's Sotyktu in a Phase 3 psoriasis head-to-head.
**What it means:** Direct-comparison wins are rare and commercially decisive — Takeda gets a clean "we beat the incumbent" story for its ~$4B bet, and BMS's oral franchise loses its edge just as it was meant to fill the gap left by older drugs going off-patent.
**Lenses:** *Commercial* — superiority data is the strongest formulary argument there is · *Strategic* — repositions Takeda in immunology · *Scientific* — validates next-gen selective TYK2.

### 3. Employers start dropping GLP-1 coverage for weight loss
**What happened:** A growing number of employers say they'll cut coverage of GLP-1s (*the gut-hormone drug class behind Ozempic/Mounjaro*) for weight loss next year, even as prescriptions climb.
**What it means:** The whole obesity market rests on *who pays*. This is the first broad payer pushback, and it bites the weight-loss use (not diabetes) hardest — exactly where Lilly's Zepbound and Novo's Wegovy compete. The debate shifts from "how big" to "how durable."
**Lenses:** *Commercial & market access* — access, not efficacy, is now the swing factor · *Financial* — pressures the volume case under both stocks · *Geopolitical/macro* — feeds the wider drug-pricing fight.

## 🦁 Lilly Watch
- **Sonefpeglutide licensing (above)** — the headline Lilly move: it widens Lilly's obesity lead and signals a "own every mechanism" strategy. *So what for Lilly:* reinforces that the obesity race is now about *portfolio breadth and access*, not a single hero drug.
- **Foundayo (oral orforglipron) momentum** — recently FDA-approved and now being pushed toward a diabetes label after beating Novo's semaglutide on blood-sugar in Phase 3; priced as low as ~$25/month with commercial insurance to drive uptake.
- **Payer headwind** — the employer GLP-1 pullback hits Zepbound directly; the oral pill helps adherence but doesn't fix the coverage math.
- **Competitor radar** — Novo won approval for a higher-dose 7.2mg injectable Wegovy; Amgen's monthly MariTide and AstraZeneca's CSPC obesity pact remain the 2027+ threats.

## On the radar
- **Incyte nears ~$2B for Star Therapeutics** (rare blood disorders) — mid-cap pipeline refill as the 2026 deal wave rolls on.
- **Summit pulls a $500M share sale** a day after announcing it — a jittery sign for biotech financing even for a watched PD-1/VEGF *bispecific (an antibody that grabs two targets at once)*.
- **Novartis del-brax** — an *antisense oligonucleotide conjugate (a short RNA-targeting molecule bolted to a delivery tag)* from the $12B Avidity deal hit its Phase 1/2 endpoint in muscle-wasting disease.
- **Novartis doubles down on molecular glues** (*small molecules that stick two proteins together to destroy a disease target*) — a bet on the next wave of "undruggable" targets.
- **China-to-global licensing** keeps surging — ADCs (*antibody–drug conjugates, targeted "guided-missile" chemo*) and bispecifics are the most-traded oncology assets.
