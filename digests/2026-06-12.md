## Top Stories

### 1. [Takeda’s $4B TYK2 drug tops Bristol Myers’ Sotyktu in head-to-head test](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/takeda-zasocitinib-sotyktu-psoriasis-trial/822500/)
**What happened:** Takeda's oral TYK2 inhibitor zasocitinib beat BMS' Sotyktu (deucravacitinib) in a head-to-head Phase 2/3 psoriasis trial, supporting Takeda's long-held belief in superiority. [4]

**What it means:** This is the first direct comparison of TYK2 inhibitors — a class that works by blocking the TYK2 enzyme (an oral anti-inflammatory mechanism) in the JAK-STAT pathway. Takeda acquired zasocitinib through its $4B Nimbus deal in 2021, and this result validates that bet. Sotyktu, approved by the FDA in 2022, was the first oral TYK2 inhibitor to market; if zasocitinib follows, Takeda could carve out a significant share of the oral psoriasis market currently dominated by injectable biologics.

**Lenses:** *Strategic* — Takeda now has a clear competitive angle vs BMS, with head-to-head data that payers and dermatologists will weigh heavily · *Commercial* — Sotyktu had modest launch uptake; zasocitinib's superiority data could accelerate adoption and challenge BMS' first-mover advantage · *Financial* — validates the $4B Nimbus acquisition, a win for Takeda's R&D portfolio after years of pipeline concerns.

### 2. [Novartis RNA drug acquired in $12B Avidity deal notches a trial win](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/novartis-del-brax-avidity-trial-data/822628/)
**What happened:** Del-brax (del-desiran), an antisense oligonucleotide conjugate (an RNA drug) for the muscle-wasting disease myotonic dystrophy type 1, met its primary endpoint in a Phase 1/2 study. Novartis acquired the drug through its $12B purchase of Avidity Biosciences. [3]

**What it means:** This is the first clinical data readout from Avidity's platform since the Novartis acquisition closed earlier in 2026. The positive result provides early validation for the deal's rationale — Avidity's antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate (AOC) platform, which delivers RNA therapeutics to muscle tissue. Myotonic dystrophy is a rare, progressive disease with no approved therapies, so any signal of efficacy is notable.

**Lenses:** *Scientific* — validates the AOC platform's ability to target muscle tissue with RNA drugs · *Strategic* — Novartis bet $12B on Avidity's platform; one positive Phase 1/2 doesn't make a pipeline, but it's a de-risking step · *Financial* — gives investors confidence that the Avidity acquisition is beginning to yield pipeline wins.

### 3. [Summit pulls $500M share sale a day after announcing it](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/summit-therapeutics-pulls-500-million-share-sale-pd1-vegf-ivonescimab/822619/)
**What happened:** Summit withdrew its proposed $500M share offering just one day after announcing it, citing "market conditions." The company's stock has struggled to break out despite recent data milestones for its PD-1/VEGF bispecific ivonescimab. [1]

**What it means:** The abrupt reversal is unusual and raises questions. Summit's ivonescimab is a leading PD-1/VEGF bispecific (a dual-targeting cancer immunotherapy) — one of the most watched assets in oncology — with data that has impressed in non-small cell lung cancer. But the share sale cancellation may signal that management felt the stock was too cheap to dilute, or that institutional demand was weaker than expected. Either way, it creates noise around a stock that had been riding high on clinical momentum.

**Lenses:** *Financial & capital markets* — sudden withdrawal from a marketed offering suggests either shareholder pushback or a view that shares are undervalued · *Strategic* — raises questions about Summit's cash runway and willingness to fund development independently vs. seeking a partner.

### 4. [An AI-designed pill just outperformed an approved Bristol drug](https://www.investors.com/news/ai-designed-drug-outperforms-bms-drug-trial)
**What happened:** An AI-designed small molecule beat an approved Bristol Myers Squibb drug in a clinical trial — one of the clearest demonstrations yet that computationally designed drugs can beat traditionally discovered ones in the clinic. [92]

**What it means:** This is a landmark moment for the AI-in-drug-discovery thesis. While multiple AI-designed molecules have entered trials, head-to-head superiority against an approved standard-of-care is rare. The result strengthens the case for platforms at companies like Recursion, Insilico, and others — and argues that AI can generate not just novel but *better* molecules.

**Lenses:** *Technology & innovation* — validates the core thesis for AI drug discovery: generating molecules with better properties, not just faster · *Competitive* — incumbents relying on traditional discovery face a structural question about their R&D productivity · *Financial* — could trigger M&A interest in AI-native biotechs.
