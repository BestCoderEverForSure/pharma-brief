## Top Stories

### 1. Takeda's zasocitinib beats BMS's Sotyktu in psoriasis showdown
**What happened:** Takeda announced that zasocitinib, its oral TYK2 inhibitor (a type of oral anti-inflammatory drug), topped Bristol Myers Squibb's Sotyktu (deucravacitinib) in a head-to-head Phase 3 trial for plaque psoriasis [4].
**What it means:** Takeda acquired zasocitinib via its $4B Nimbus Therapeutics deal and has long argued it's best-in-class. This head-to-head win gives the commercial team a potent message vs. Sotyktu, which launched to slower-than-expected uptake.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* — a direct competitive blow to BMS's Sotyktu franchise, which was already struggling with payer access. *Commercial* — if the data translates to a label advantage, Takeda could capture meaningful share in the oral psoriasis market. *Financial* — validates the $4B Nimbus acquisition thesis.

### 2. Novartis' del-brax meets primary endpoint — first proof point from $12B Avidity deal
**What happened:** Novartis reported that del-brax, an "antisense oligonucleotide conjugate" (a targeted RNA medicine) acquired in the $12B Avidity Biosciences deal, met its primary endpoint in a Phase 1/2 study for a muscle-wasting disease [3].
**What it means:** This is the first clinical data from Novartis's largest recent bolt-on acquisition. Positive early data de-risks the platform thesis — that delivering RNA drugs to muscle tissue works — and supports the broader deal rationale.
**Lenses:** *Scientific* — oligonucleotide conjugates are a technically challenging modality; positive Phase 1/2 is a meaningful derisking event. *Strategic* — validates Avidity's platform and could accelerate Novartis's pipeline in neuromuscular disease. *Financial* — the $12B price tag looked rich; this data helps justify it.

### 3. Summit Therapeutics pulls $500M share sale after one day
**What happened:** Summit Therapeutics withdrew its proposed $500M public offering of shares the day after announcing it, citing "market conditions" [1].
**What it means:** Summit is the developer of ivonescimab, a leading PD-1/VEGF bispecific (a dual-targeting cancer antibody) partnered from China's Akeso. The stock has struggled to sustain momentum after data readouts, and pulling an overnight offering suggests underwhelming investor demand — a warning sign for the company's capital needs.
**Lenses:** *Financial* — signals weak market appetite for a follow-on at current valuation. *Strategic* — leaves Summit with less cash to fund ivonescimab's expensive Phase 3 program. *Geopolitical* — comes amid renewed US-China biotech tensions (WuXi lawsuit [63]; Bloomberg op-ed [72]).

### 4. FDA approves bemotrizinol — first new US sunscreen ingredient in decades
**What happened:** The FDA approved bemotrizinol, a broad-spectrum UV filter long used in Europe and Asia, as a new sunscreen active ingredient for the US market [17][32][110].
**What it means:** The US sunscreen market has been notoriously slow to approve new ingredients — this marks the first novel UV filter cleared in over 20 years. Bemotrizinol offers better UVA protection and stability than many existing US-approved filters. RFK Jr. touted the approval process that led to the decision [104].
**Lenses:** *Regulatory* — a notable shift in FDA approach to sunscreen ingredients, potentially opening the door for additional foreign-approved filters. *Commercial* — major opportunity for sunscreen manufacturers to reformulate products with a superior ingredient.
