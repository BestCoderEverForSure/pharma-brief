## Top Stories

### 1. [Medicare Sets 2028 Prices for 13 New Drugs Under Biden-Era Law](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/medicare-sets-2028-prices-13-new-drugs-under-biden-era-law-2026-11-25/)
**What happened:** Medicare has announced the 2028 negotiated prices for 13 additional drugs under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), expanding the number of medicines subject to cost controls. This move particularly targets drugs nearing or past patent expiration [1] [123].
**What it means:** This expansion signals the growing financial impact of the IRA on pharmaceutical companies, potentially accelerating revenue erosion for established products. Companies will need to adjust long-term pricing strategies and R&D investment decisions, especially for assets with extended market exclusivity.
**Lenses:** *Financial* - Increased pricing pressure on older drugs, impacting revenue forecasts and potentially accelerating generic/biosimilar erosion. · *Regulatory* - Demonstrates the CMS's continued implementation of the IRA, setting a precedent for future price negotiations.

### 2. [Bayer Signs $3.25 Billion Oncology Deal with Hansoh Pharma](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/chinas-hansoh-pharma-strikes-drug-deal-with-bayer-unit-worth-nearly-33-billion-2026-11-25/)
**What happened:** Bayer has entered an exclusive worldwide licensing agreement with China's Hansoh Pharma for the Phase 1 oncology candidate HS-20122, an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC). The deal is potentially valued at up to $3.25 billion [7] [209] [218] [219] [235].
**What it means:** This substantial investment highlights Bayer's strategic focus on strengthening its oncology pipeline with advanced modalities like ADCs, which are seen as a key growth area in cancer treatment. It also underscores the increasing role of Chinese biopharma in global drug discovery and development partnerships.
**Lenses:** *Strategic* - Diversifies Bayer's oncology portfolio and gains access to a promising ADC platform. · *Financial* - A significant upfront and milestone-based deal, reflecting the high value placed on innovative early-stage oncology assets.

### 3. [FDA Approves Cogent’s Drug for Rare Stomach Cancer](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/25/fda-approves-cogent-drug-rare-stomach-cancer/)
**What happened:** Cogent Biosciences received FDA approval for its drug, Ekitna (bezuclastinib), in combination with Pfizer's Sutent, for the treatment of gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) after imatinib failure [3] [10] [15] [75]. This marks Cogent's first drug approval.
**What it means:** This approval provides a new treatment option for patients with a rare and aggressive form of stomach cancer, particularly those who have progressed on prior therapies. For Cogent, it's a critical validation of its pipeline and a transition to a commercial-stage company, opening doors for future development and partnerships.
**Lenses:** *Scientific & Clinical* - Offers a targeted therapy for a difficult-to-treat cancer, addressing an unmet need. · *Commercial* - Establishes Cogent's market presence and revenue stream, crucial for a newly approved biotech.
